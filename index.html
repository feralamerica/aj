Anand Jon: The Pipeline

Anand Jon Alexander is a fashion designer serving fifty-nine years to life in California for sexually abusing young women and girls he lured with modeling promises. This site collects the public record of his crimes and convictions, the coverage of them, and the documented network of people and organizations now seeking his release, including a website aimed at girls that presents him as a mentor.

The crimes and the convictions

Everything in this section is a matter of public court record and contemporaneous news reporting.

Documented Court records or a subject's own published words.

California, 2008 Documented

On November 13, 2008, a Los Angeles jury convicted Anand Jon Alexander on sixteen counts, including forcible rape, sexual battery, and contributing to the delinquency of minors, tied to seven young accusers, some assaulted at fourteen. The jury acquitted him on four felonies and deadlocked on three. In 2009, Superior Court Judge David Wesley sentenced him to fifty-nine years to life, saying he had shown no remorse. Thirteen women were present in the courtroom. (LA Superior Court case BA327190.)

New York, 2013 Documented

Facing an initial set of roughly forty-nine charges, Jon pleaded guilty in 2013 to a single count of criminal sexual act involving a woman lured with modeling promises. The agreed term amounted to time already served.

Texas, 2007 Documented

A Dallas grand jury (292nd Judicial District) returned three sexual-assault indictments, cases F-0772363, F-0772364, and F-0772365, reaching back to alleged offenses in 2002, 2004, and 2005. Warrants issued in 2007. He was never separately tried, because the California sentence subsumed the rest.

The predator and his prey

Drawn from prosecutors' descriptions and accusers' accounts as reported at trial.

Prosecutors described a consistent pattern. Jon contacted aspiring models, many of them teenagers, many from small towns, through social media and email. He complimented their look, promised photo shoots and industry introductions, and paid for their travel to Los Angeles or New York. Once a young woman was isolated in his residence, far from anyone she knew, he assaulted her. Reporting at the time placed the number of accusers across the states at around twenty. He has consistently maintained the encounters were consensual.

At sentencing, accusers addressed the court directly. One, assaulted at fourteen, described how he had taken her adolescence, her trust, and her dream. Documented

The alliances: one core, many banners

The effort to free Anand Jon does not run through a single organization. It runs through a cluster of similarly named nonprofits, an awards forum, a media outlet, and a youth-facing brand, staffed by the same small group of people. Each one presents him as a cause.

Documented  A charity the IRS delisted, still asking for money. American Justice Alliance (EIN 82-3588588, Stockton, California) had its 501(c)(3) status automatically revoked by the IRS on May 15, 2020 for failing to file Form 990 returns for three consecutive years. The IRS record shows no reinstatement. Its website nonetheless still states, "We are a 501(c)(3) nonprofit organization, approved by the IRS ... as a tax-exempt, charitable organization," and carries a "Donate Here Now" button.

A fairness note

These alliances also feature genuinely innocent exonerees and legitimate advocates, among them Obie Anthony, Jeffrey Deskovic, and Brian Banks. They are not implicated by appearing here. The documented point is narrower: a convicted sex offender's release cause has been placed inside a network of otherwise-legitimate justice advocacy, run by a shared core of people. Separately, several prominent public figures appear in these groups' materials; being listed is not evidence of endorsement, and none are characterized here as supporting his release.

How it continues from behind bars

The through-line of this story is that the pressure on his victims did not stop at his conviction. It moved to other people.

Documented In November 2009, an accuser who went public as "Autumn A" described what happened after Jon was imprisoned. His supporters, she said, found her on Facebook and approached her friends, offering a reward of one hundred thousand dollars for information that could help exonerate him. "The day I saw the page on Facebook, my heart dropped," she said. Her account helped prompt California legislators to pursue a law addressing technology-enabled intimidation of victims.

Fifteen years later, the same pattern, pressure and outreach conducted through supporters rather than by the imprisoned man himself, is the reason this site exists: to document who is doing that work, and in whose name.

Standards, fairness, and corrections

How this record is built and maintained.

Evidence standard

Items marked Documented are drawn from court records, government filings, or the subjects' own published statements. Items marked Under investigation are attributed allegations or leads that have not been confirmed to a primary source; they are presented as allegations, never as established fact. Convictions cited here are matters of public record.

The subjects' position

Anand Jon Alexander maintains that he is innocent and has pursued appeals. International Justice Alliance states that it works on behalf of the wrongfully convicted, and it lists genuine exonerees among its causes. Individuals and organizations named here are invited to respond; requests for comment are outstanding, and responses will be published as they are received.
